\title{When Does Post-Pruning Fine-Tuning Help?
Temporal Operating-Point Sensitivity in Text-to-Audio Diffusion Evaluation}

\begin{abstract}
Structured pruning of a text-to-audio diffusion model is usually followed by recovery
fine-tuning, and the benefit of that fine-tuning is typically summarized by a single
aggregate score at one inference setting. We show that, for the published pruned and
post-fine-tuning checkpoints of AudioLDM-M-Full, the measured advantage of the
post-fine-tuning checkpoint over the pruned checkpoint depends strongly on the temporal
evaluation operating point. Using a paired, common-noise design with prompt-level
bootstrap confidence intervals, we define a temporal interaction $J$ as the difference
between the fine-tuning advantage evaluated at the model's native $10.24$\,s scale and at
a shorter $3.84$\,s scale. A pre-registered domain-specialization hypothesis fails at the
matched short operating point. The interaction $J$ is positive but borderline at a milder
pruning severity, and is prospectively confirmed at a stronger severity on a disjoint
evaluation set ($J=+0.159$, $95\%$~CI~$\ci{+0.131}{+0.187}$), robust to a pruning-seam
convention. The advantage is nonetheless still present at the short operating point, so it
is not a $10$\,s-only effect, and a severity-1 music-negative pattern does not replicate.
An independent frame-level grounding evaluator corroborates a broad native-duration gain
but rejects a simple late-event explanation. We conclude that recovery should not be
treated as a checkpoint-intrinsic scalar and should be evaluated across inference
operating points. We report explicit negatives and state that no matched dense
fine-tuned control and no human evaluation are available, so the claims are about
evaluation, not causal mechanism or perceptual quality.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
text-to-audio generation, diffusion models, structured pruning, fine-tuning, evaluation,
CLAP.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Structured pruning followed by recovery fine-tuning is a standard route to cheaper
generative diffusion models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,kim2024bksdm}, including for
text-to-audio (TTA) generation with AudioLDM~\cite{liu2023audioldm,singh2026pruning}.
The effect of the recovery stage is normally reported as the restoration of an aggregate
benchmark number, such as Fr\'echet Audio Distance or a KL divergence on an in-domain test
set at a fixed sampler setting.

A generative model, however, is queried at an inference operating point---clip duration,
sampler steps, guidance---and there is no guarantee that a single aggregate number
characterizes the fine-tuned checkpoint at every operating point a user might choose. In
this paper we ask a narrow, empirical question: \emph{does the advantage of a
post-pruning fine-tuned checkpoint over the pruned checkpoint depend on the temporal
evaluation operating point?} We study this for the publicly released pruned and
post-fine-tuning checkpoints of AudioLDM-M-Full, treating the released checkpoints as the
object of study rather than proposing a new pruning or fine-tuning method.

Our contribution is an evaluation, not a mechanism. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}\raggedright
\item A paired, common-noise evaluation with prompt-level bootstrap that isolates the
      post-fine-tuning$-$pruned contrast at two temporal operating points, and a temporal
      interaction $J$ that quantifies how that contrast changes with duration.
\item A prospectively frozen, cross-severity test: the interaction is borderline at a
      milder pruning severity and is confirmed at a stronger severity on a disjoint
      evaluation set with new seeds, robust to a pruning-seam convention.
\item Honest negatives---a pre-registered domain-specialization hypothesis fails, the
      advantage at the short point does not vanish, a severity-1 music-negative pattern does
      not replicate, and an independent diagnostic rejects a simple late-event
      explanation---together with the single limitation (no matched dense fine-tuned
      control) that bounds the claim to evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\textbf{Diffusion pruning and recovery.} Structural pruning of diffusion U-Nets with
fine-tuning recovery has been studied for image models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,
kim2024bksdm} and, for TTA, by Singh et al.~\cite{singh2026pruning}, who prune an
AudioLDM-family U-Net and recover with fine-tuning on the AudioCaps training set, reporting
in-domain FAD/KL and event-capture metrics at a fixed sampler. These works evaluate
recovery at a single operating point; we instead vary the operating point and measure the
change in the fine-tuning advantage.
\textbf{Compression and inference regime.} Compression error is known to interact with the
reverse diffusion trajectory and the number of sampling steps. We are not aware of prior work
establishing, for structural pruning of a TTA model, an \emph{evaluation-time} interaction
between the recovery advantage and the temporal operating point.
\textbf{TTA evaluation.} CLAP similarity~\cite{wu2023clap} is a standard automatic proxy
for text--audio alignment; distributional metrics such as FAD~\cite{kilgour2019fad} and
event classifiers such as PANNs~\cite{kong2020panns} are common but sample-size and
encoder sensitive. These limits motivate a paired, multi-metric treatment.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Systems and pruning severities}
The dense model is AudioLDM-M-Full~\cite{liu2023audioldm}, a latent-diffusion U-Net with
\texttt{channel\_mult}~$=(1,2,3,5)$ and $\approx\!416$\,M U-Net parameters, itself AudioCaps-
specialized by the original authors. We use the publicly released L1 structurally pruned
checkpoints at two severities, denoted by the pruned per-stage channel multipliers: a
milder \emph{severity~1} $(1,2,3,1)$ ($145.7$\,M U-Net parameters, $-65.0\%$) and a stronger
\emph{severity~2} $(1,2,1,1)$ ($\approx\!83\%$ U-Net reduction). Each severity prunes the
deeper U-Net stages more aggressively. We verified that our pruning operator reproduces the
released pruned weights bit-exactly (per-tensor oracle checks). We do not train any system.

\subsection{Post-pruning fine-tuning checkpoints}
The \emph{post-fine-tuning checkpoint} at each severity is the released checkpoint obtained
by fine-tuning the pruned U-Net on AudioCaps for $10^{6}$ steps~\cite{singh2026pruning}; we
load it and materialize its exponential-moving-average (EMA) weights, and verify its
checksum. We refer to the pruned checkpoint and the post-fine-tuning checkpoint throughout;
the recovery fine-tuning is AudioCaps fine-tuning of the pruned U-Net.

\subsection{Evaluation batteries and operating points}
The in-domain battery is AudioCaps \textsc{test} captions; the out-of-domain battery is a
held-out music set of hip-hop-style captions derived from a MusicCaps-style
source~\cite{agostinelli2023musiclm}, with near-zero exposure to that subdomain in the
recovery corpus. We generate at two temporal operating points: a \emph{short} point
($3.84$\,s, latent length $96$) and the fine-tuning-native \emph{native} point ($10.24$\,s,
latent length $256$). All generation uses DDIM with $50$ steps, guidance $2.5$, $\eta=0$,
\texttt{fp32}, single generation, EMA weights. The music battery is generated only at the
short point (its recipe).

The severity-2 evaluation uses \emph{disjoint} sets constructed after the severity-1
results were known: $192$ AudioCaps prompts that exclude the severity-1 prompts, the
training set and the severity-1 music set, and $64$ new music prompts with zero overlap with
the severity-1 music set, all under new selection salts and generation seeds. Severity-1
uses $96$ AudioCaps prompts for the controlled reversal test, a matched $80$-prompt subset
for the temporal comparison, and $64$ music prompts.

\subsection{Paired design, scoring, and statistics}
For each prompt and operating point, the compared systems receive identical generation
noise $x_T$ (common random numbers), so a per-prompt contrast is not confounded by sampling
noise. The primary scorer is CLAP (\texttt{laion/clap-htsat-fused}, revision
\texttt{365dea6e})~\cite{wu2023clap}, applied with a fixed batch, order and seed convention
that is part of the endpoint definition. The statistical unit is the prompt; the three
music replicates are averaged to a per-prompt contrast before analysis. Confidence
intervals are $95\%$ prompt-level percentile bootstrap ($B=10^{4}$).

\subsection{Estimands and gates}
We first ask, at a single operating point, how the fine-tuned checkpoint compares to the
pruned one. Let $R(\text{context})$ be the mean per-prompt CLAP cosine of the
post-fine-tuning checkpoint minus the pruned checkpoint in that context; positive values
favour the post-fine-tuning checkpoint. We write $\Rn$, $\Rs$ and $\Rm$ for the AudioCaps
native, AudioCaps short and held-out music contrasts.

The central quantity is whether the fine-tuning advantage \emph{changes} with temporal
scale. We define the temporal interaction
\begin{equation}
J \;=\; \Rn \;-\; \Rs ,
\end{equation}
the difference between the advantage at the native and short operating points, estimated as
a paired per-prompt contrast. A pre-specified gate declares the interaction resolved when
the lower $95\%$ bound satisfies $\mathrm{lo}_{95}(J)>0$. We also report a context contrast
$K=\Rn-\Rm$; because it changes both the prompt domain and the duration at once, we treat
$K$ as a bundled context effect, not a pure-domain effect. Sign-of-effect gates use a
smallest effect size of interest $\text{SESOI}=0.025$; a short-operating-point equivalence
test asks whether the $90\%$ CI of $\Rs$ lies within $[-0.025,+0.025]$.

\subsection{Evidence classes}
We separate: (i)~\emph{prospectively frozen primary} results (the severity-2 replication and
its seam sensitivity; the pre-registered severity-1 negative), whose protocol and gates were
frozen before scoring; (ii)~a \emph{prospectively specified follow-up} (the severity-1
temporal comparison); (iii)~\emph{secondary} corroboration (a second CLAP-family scorer, and
KL/event-count metrics named in the frozen protocol but implemented after the primary
result); and (iv)~a \emph{post-result diagnostic} (frame-level grounding). Cross-severity
magnitude comparisons are exploratory and confounded (different experiments, prompts, sample
sizes and checkpoints) and are never promoted to confirmatory evidence.

\section{Results}

\subsection{A controlled test rejects domain specialization}
A severity-1 music observation motivated a strict hypothesis: that fine-tuning trades
in-domain for out-of-domain alignment (a recovery reversal). We tested it prospectively at
the matched short operating point ($3.84$\,s). It fails: the in-domain contrast is
essentially zero, $R_{\text{AC}}=-0.002$ ($n=96$; Table~\ref{tab:core}), so there is no
in-domain sacrifice to trade, and the pre-registered gate does not pass. This reframed the
work from a mechanism claim to an evaluation question. The held-out music contrast at this
severity is negative, $\Rm=-0.094$.

\subsection{A temporal operating-point hypothesis}
The recovery fine-tuning operates at a native temporal scale of $10.24$\,s (latent length
$256$), whereas the controlled test above used $3.84$\,s (latent length $96$). If the
apparent absence of an advantage is specific to the short operating point, then evaluating
at the native scale should change the picture. We therefore compare the fine-tuning
advantage at the two temporal operating points directly.

\subsection{Severity 1: a larger native advantage, borderline interaction}
On a matched $80$-prompt subset, changing only the temporal extent yields a near-zero short
advantage, $\Rs=+0.008$, and a resolved native advantage, $\Rn=+0.052$
(Table~\ref{tab:core}). The interaction is positive but borderline,
$J=+0.044$ $\ci{-0.001}{+0.087}$: the $95\%$ CI narrowly includes zero, so the gate does not
pass, and we report it as directional evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}a). A second
CLAP-family scorer gives a resolved interaction in the same direction ($J=+0.075$), but it is
a corroborative secondary, not the primary endpoint.

\subsection{Severity 2: a prospective replication}
We then tested the pattern prospectively at the stronger severity~2 on the disjoint
evaluation sets, with the estimands and gates frozen before any severity-2 output was
scored. The interaction is clearly positive and resolved,
$J=+0.159$ $\ci{+0.131}{+0.187}$, driven by a large native-duration advantage,
$\Rn=+0.244$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}b, Table~\ref{tab:core}). The context contrast is
also resolved, $K=+0.235$. Both $J$ and $K$ are
robust to the pruning-seam convention: an alternative baseline that reproduces the released
checkpoint's decoder-seam convention (differing in exactly three tensors) gives
$J=+0.161$ and $K=+0.238$, with the same gate outcomes. The primary severity-2 baseline is
the convention consistent with severity~1; the seam alternative is a pre-specified
sensitivity analysis and never rescues a failed primary gate.

\subsection{The advantage is present, not absent, at the short point}
The interaction does not mean ``fine-tuning helps only at $10$\,s.'' At severity~2 the short
advantage is itself resolved and positive, $\Rs=+0.085$, and the equivalence test fails
($90\%$ CI $\ci{+0.069}{+0.102}$, outside the $\pm0.025$ margin): the advantage is smaller at
the short point, not gone. The specific severity-1 music-negative pattern also does not
replicate: at severity~2 the music contrast is approximately null, $\Rm=+0.009$
(Table~\ref{tab:core}). Thus the context and temporal dependence replicate, but the exact
severity-1 sign pattern does not. The stronger effects at severity~2
are consistent with a severity dependence, but a cross-severity comparison mixes two
experiments, prompt sets and checkpoints and is treated as exploratory only.

\subsection{Corroboration}
We use secondary metrics conservatively; CLAP is the primary endpoint. At the severity-2
native point, a second CLAP-family scorer agrees on a large advantage ($\Rn=+0.375$,
$J=+0.185$; both CIs exclude $0$) but is not human evaluation; on held-out music this scorer
shows a small negative contrast ($\Rm=-0.037$) that the primary CLAP does not, with no gate
role, so the music sign pattern remains unestablished. Two pre-specified event-level metrics, implemented after the primary result,
also favour the post-fine-tuning checkpoint with paired CIs excluding zero
(Table~\ref{tab:secondary}): a lower KL to real references and a higher count of PANN
top-$10$ captured ground-truth labels. Distribution-level FAD and FD point in the same
direction but are single scalars per system without paired CIs, so we report them as
descriptive.

\subsection{Frame-level diagnostic}
As a prospectively frozen post-result diagnostic, an independent frame-level evaluator
(a non-CLAP audio--text grounding model) measures where in the clip the requested events are
grounded. At the native scale the post-fine-tuning checkpoint shows a large increase in
frame-level grounding of requested events across the whole clip (semantic mass
$+0.27$~$\ci{+0.22}{+0.33}$, occupancy $+0.27$, quarter-coverage $+0.38$, peak $+0.41$; CIs
exclude $0$; both severities; seam-robust). But the gain is not concentrated late: the
late-minus-early difference is essentially zero, $T=-0.002$ $\ci{-0.024}{+0.020}$ at
severity~2 (gate fails; $-0.020$ at severity~1, point not positive). The diagnostic therefore
supports a broad native-duration grounding gain and rejects a simple explanation in which
the benefit comes from events being shifted into the late portion of the clip. It is
frame-level grounding evidence, not calibrated event probability, local causal activation, or
perceptual quality.

\section{Discussion}
The advantage of post-pruning fine-tuning over the pruned checkpoint is not a single number:
in our automatic evaluation it depends on the temporal operating point and the evaluation
context, and it is heterogeneous across prompts (some favour the pruned checkpoint on both
CLAP and the frame-level diagnostic). The methodological implication is that recovery should
not be treated as a checkpoint-intrinsic scalar: recovery claims for a generative model
should be evaluated across several inference operating points rather than at one aggregate
setting. We state this as an implication for evaluation, not a universal law---our study
covers one model family, two pruning severities and one temporal axis.

\section{Limitations}
The main limitation is that mechanistic attribution is blocked. The ideal control---a dense,
unpruned model given a matched AudioCaps fine-tune---is unavailable (the checkpoint was
confirmed deleted), and the public dense text-fine-tuned release is not an equivalent
substitute (different data and recipe, dense start). We therefore cannot attribute the
observed dependence to pruning specifically, and we do not claim that fine-tuning restores
the pruned model, that it restores dense performance, or that it consistently improves
individual samples; the dense reference (Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}a) neither resolves a
residual gap to dense nor establishes equivalence to it. The primary scorer family is CLAP
(the second scorer is CLAP-derived), and there is no human evaluation: a designed listening
study was cancelled before launch with zero participants, so it contributes no evidence, and
the public audio examples are illustrative only. The operating point differs from the
published recipe (DDIM $50$ vs.\ $200$ steps), so external validity to that recipe is
untested although both systems are scored identically. The cross-severity comparison is
exploratory and confounded, and the frame-level diagnostic is a post-result grounding
measure, native-duration only.

\section{Conclusion}
For the released pruned and post-fine-tuning checkpoints of AudioLDM-M-Full, the advantage of
fine-tuning over pruning depends on the temporal evaluation operating point: an interaction
that is borderline at a milder pruning severity is prospectively confirmed at a stronger
severity on a disjoint set, robust to a pruning-seam convention, present at the short point,
and corroborated by an independent frame-level measure. Post-pruning recovery is better
described as multi-operating-point behaviour than as a single restored score. Companion audio
examples are online.\footnote{\url{https://gbibbo.github.io/audioldm-modality-swap-pruning/}}
