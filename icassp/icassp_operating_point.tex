\title{Recovery Fine-Tuning Recovers Where It Was Trained:\\
Duration- and Domain-Dependent Gains in Pruned Text-to-Audio Diffusion}

\twoauthors
  {Gabriel Bibb\'o}
  {Independent researcher \\
   \texttt{gabobibbo@gmail.com}}
  {Arshdeep Singh, \, Mark D. Plumbley}
  {King's College London, UK \\
   \{\texttt{arshdeep.singh}, \texttt{mark.plumbley}\}\texttt{@kcl.ac.uk}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Structured pruning of text-to-audio diffusion models is followed by recovery fine-tuning, whose benefit
is usually certified by one score at one inference setting. Using the released pruned and fine-tuned
AudioLDM-M checkpoints at two pruning severities ($65\%$ and $83\%$ of U-Net parameters removed), we
measure the recovery gain in text--audio alignment with a paired, common-noise protocol across clip
duration and prompt domain. The gain is large at the fine-tuning duration of $10.24$\,s (CLAP
$0.055\!\to\!0.299$ at $83\%$ pruning), several-fold smaller at $3.84$\,s ($0.015\!\to\!0.100$), and
absent or negative on held-out music. The duration dependence was pre-specified, replicates on a
disjoint prompt set, survives a family-wise correction, rank-based statistics and the pruning-seam
convention, and is corroborated by a second scorer, event-level metrics and a frame-level grounding
model. A matched dense control shows that the fine-tuned checkpoint responds to clip duration at least
as strongly as the unpruned model, whereas the $83\%$-pruned checkpoint barely responds at all. Recovery
should be reported across operating points; lacking a matched dense fine-tuned control and human
ratings, our claims concern evaluation, not mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
text-to-audio generation, diffusion models, structured pruning, recovery fine-tuning, evaluation
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Structured pruning followed by recovery fine-tuning is the standard route to cheaper diffusion
models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,kim2024bksdm,fang2025tinyfusion}, recently applied to text-to-audio
(TTA) generation with AudioLDM~\cite{liu2023audioldm,singh2026pruning}. The recovery stage is judged by
the restoration of one aggregate number---a Fr\'echet distance or a KL divergence on the in-domain test
set at one sampler setting. A generative model, however, is used at an inference \emph{operating point}:
a clip duration, a prompt domain, a sampler configuration. Nothing guarantees that a recovery gain
measured at one point describes the fine-tuned checkpoint at another.

We ask: \emph{does the gain of recovery fine-tuning over the pruned checkpoint depend on the
operating point at which it is evaluated?} We take the released pruned and fine-tuned AudioLDM-M-Full
checkpoints of Singh et al.~\cite{singh2026pruning} at two pruning severities as the object of study,
train nothing, and measure the paired recovery gain in text--audio alignment across two clip durations
and two prompt domains. Our findings are:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt \topsep2pt
\item \textbf{Duration.} The recovery gain is several times larger at the fine-tuning duration
      ($10.24$\,s) than at $3.84$\,s. The interaction was pre-specified, replicates on a disjoint
      prompt set at the stronger severity, survives a family-wise correction, and holds on scale-free
      statistics. Against a matched dense control, the $83\%$-pruned checkpoint has lost most of the
      dense model's response to clip duration; the fine-tuned checkpoint responds like the dense model.
\item \textbf{Domain.} At matched duration the gain is present in-domain (AudioCaps) and absent or
      negative on held-out music, at both severities and, at severity~2, at both durations. This is
      not a scoring floor: the pruned checkpoint scores higher on music than in-domain at $10.24$\,s.
\item \textbf{Where the gain lives.} A frame-level grounding model shows the native-duration gain
      spread uniformly over the clip, and a crop analysis shows the short-duration deficit arises from
      \emph{generating} short clips, not from scoring short excerpts: the first $3.84$\,s of a native
      generation already carries the whole gain at severity~1 and over half of it at severity~2.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:related}
\rwhead{Pruning and recovery in diffusion models.}
Structural pruning of diffusion backbones with fine-tuning recovery is established for image
models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,kim2024bksdm,fang2025tinyfusion}. For TTA, Singh et
al.~\cite{singh2026pruning} L1-prune the AudioLDM U-Net, fine-tune on AudioCaps for $10^{6}$ steps and
report in-domain FAD/KL at a fixed sampler; notably their fine-tuned pruned model \emph{surpasses} the
unpruned model in-domain (FAD $1.57$ vs.\ $3.95$), a first sign that the recovery stage also acts as
further in-domain specialisation. All these works evaluate recovery at one operating point.

\rwhead{Evaluating text-to-audio generation.}
CLAP similarity~\cite{wu2023clap} is the standard automatic proxy for text--audio alignment;
distribution metrics such as FAD~\cite{kilgour2019fad} are sensitive to sample size and embedding
choice~\cite{gui2024fad,chung2025kad}; event classifiers~\cite{kong2020panns}, human-aligned
CLAP~\cite{takano2025humanclap} and frame-level grounding models~\cite{li2026finelap} measure other
axes, motivating a paired, multi-metric design with one pre-specified primary endpoint.

\rwhead{Duration as an operating point.}
Latent audio diffusion conditions generation on the latent length and is trained at a fixed duration
($10.24$\,s for AudioLDM~\cite{liu2023audioldm,liu2024audioldm2}; newer systems condition on timing
explicitly~\cite{evans2024stableaudioopen}), so duration is a mechanistically motivated factor for a
checkpoint fine-tuned at one duration; fine-tuning also trades in-distribution gains for
out-of-distribution losses~\cite{kumar2022finetuning}, motivating the domain axis.

\section{Experimental design}
\subsection{Systems}
The dense model is AudioLDM-M-Full~\cite{liu2023audioldm} (U-Net stage multipliers
$(1,2,3,5)$, $415.96$\,M U-Net parameters), itself AudioCaps-fine-tuned by its authors. We study the
two released pruning severities of~\cite{singh2026pruning}: \emph{severity~1}, $(1,2,3,1)$,
$145.67$\,M parameters ($-65.0\%$), and \emph{severity~2}, $(1,2,1,1)$, $71.08$\,M ($-82.9\%$; the
authors report $39\%$ fewer multiply--accumulates at this budget). At each severity we compare the
\emph{pruned checkpoint} (P) with the \emph{fine-tuned checkpoint} (\PFT), the released result of
$10^{6}$ steps of AudioCaps recovery fine-tuning of P~\cite{singh2026pruning} (EMA weights, checksums
verified). Since the released pruned checkpoints carry no usable EMA weights, P is the released
L1 channel selection applied to the dense EMA weights: at severity~1 this reproduces the released raw
pruned weights bit-exactly ($690/690$ tensors); at severity~2 the released checkpoint differs in three
decoder-seam tensors, so both conventions are evaluated (A$'$ primary, B$'$ sensitivity). ``Recovery''
names the fine-tuning stage, not an achieved restoration; we train no system.

\subsection{Operating points and prompt batteries}
Two durations: the \emph{native} point ($10.24$\,s, latent length $256$; the fine-tuning duration) and a
\emph{short} point ($3.84$\,s, latent length $96$). Two domains: in-domain AudioCaps \textsc{test}
captions~\cite{kim2019audiocaps} and held-out hip-hop/rap captions from
MusicCaps~\cite{agostinelli2023musiclm}, a sub-domain with near-zero exposure in the recovery corpus.
Severity~2 uses $192$ AudioCaps prompts (disjoint from all severity-1 prompts) and $64$ music prompts
($3$ replicates at $3.84$\,s, one at $10.24$\,s); severity~1 uses $96$ AudioCaps prompts for the
pre-specified domain test, a matched $80$-prompt subset for the duration comparison (on which the dense
model is also generated at both durations, giving a matched dense control), and $64$ music prompts at
$3.84$\,s. Generation uses DDIM~\cite{song2021ddim}, $50$ steps, guidance $2.5$, $\eta=0$,
\texttt{fp32}, single generation, EMA weights (off the published recipe, Sec.~5).

\subsection{Paired protocol and scoring}
Within each operating point the compared systems receive identical generation noise $x_T$ per prompt
(common random numbers), so a per-prompt contrast is not confounded by sampling noise; across durations
the latent shapes differ, so duration contrasts are prompt-paired but not noise-paired. The primary
scorer is fused CLAP (\texttt{laion/\allowbreak clap-htsat-fused}, rev.\ \texttt{365dea6e})~\cite{wu2023clap},
which repeat-pads $3.84$\,s audio to $10$\,s and centre-crops $10.24$\,s audio to $10$\,s; every
(system, operating point) is scored with one fixed-order, fixed-seed call, part of the endpoint
(re-scoring the same clips in another batch shifts means by $\le0.002$). The unit is the prompt (music replicates averaged first); intervals are
$95\%$ prompt-level percentile bootstrap intervals ($B=10^{4}$).

\subsection{Quantities and analysis plan}
Let $R$ be the mean per-prompt recovery gain, CLAP(\PFT) $-$ CLAP(P), at a given (domain, duration);
$\Rn$, $\Rs$ and $\Rm$ denote AudioCaps-native, AudioCaps-short and music-short. The
\emph{duration interaction} is
\begin{equation}
J \;=\; \Rn - \Rs \;=\; s(\PFT) - s(\mathrm{P}),
\end{equation}
where $s(\cdot)$ is a system's \emph{duration response}, its mean per-prompt score change from $3.84$ to
$10.24$\,s; $J$ is a paired per-prompt contrast with pre-specified gate $\mathrm{lo}_{95}(J)>0$. The
\emph{domain contrast} at matched duration is $\Rs-\Rm$; the smallest effect size of interest is
$0.025$. ``Pre-specified'' means that estimand, gate and prompt set were committed to the
version-controlled repository before any score was seen (Table~\ref{tab:plan}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The recovery gain grows with clip duration}
Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction} and Table~\ref{tab:core} give the primary result. At severity~2, on the
disjoint $192$-prompt set with estimands frozen before scoring, the interaction is clearly resolved,
$J=+0.159$~$\ci{+0.131}{+0.187}$: fine-tuning raises CLAP alignment from $0.055$ to $0.299$ at the native
duration ($\Rn=+0.244$) but only from $0.015$ to $0.100$ at $3.84$\,s ($\Rs=+0.085$). The two duration
responses tell the story: the pruned checkpoint gains just $s(\mathrm{P})=+0.040$~$\ci{+0.029}{+0.052}$
from the longer clip, the fine-tuned one $s(\PFT)=+0.200$~$\ci{+0.173}{+0.226}$. The result is
seam-robust (B$'$: $J=+0.161$). The gain is smaller at the short point, not absent ($\Rs$ resolved;
$90\%$ CI $\ci{+0.069}{+0.102}$ outside $\pm0.025$).

At the milder severity~1 the same pattern appears with smaller effects: $\Rs=+0.008$
$\ci{-0.023}{+0.039}$, $\Rn=+0.052$~$\ci{+0.009}{+0.093}$, $J=+0.044$~$\ci{-0.001}{+0.087}$, whose
interval narrowly includes zero. Here the matched dense control anchors the scale. On the same $80$
prompts, scored under the same convention, the dense model's duration response is
$s(\mathrm{dense})=+0.150$~$\ci{+0.100}{+0.198}$ ($0.202\!\to\!0.352$), indistinguishable from the
pruned checkpoint's $s(\mathrm{P})=+0.149$~$\ci{+0.119}{+0.178}$ (paired difference $-0.001$
$\ci{-0.056}{+0.055}$), while the fine-tuned checkpoint responds more, $s(\PFT)=+0.193$~$\ci{+0.152}{+0.232}$,
exceeding dense by $+0.043$ $\ci{-0.020}{+0.109}$. In relative terms, fine-tuning closes $8\%$
$\ci{-30\%}{+36\%}$ of the pruned checkpoint's gap to dense at $3.84$\,s and $52\%$ $\ci{+11\%}{+103\%}$ at
$10.24$\,s. At severity~2, $s(\mathrm{P})=+0.040$ is far below the dense response measured on the
severity-1 prompts, while $s(\PFT)=+0.200$ is of dense magnitude (a cross-set, descriptive comparison).

Because every system scores higher at $10.24$\,s, a raw-scale interaction could reflect scale alone, so
we report it on scale-free statistics as well (post-hoc). The fraction $W$ of prompts on which \PFT{}
beats P rises from $0.44$ to $0.64$ at severity~1 ($\Delta W=+0.20$~$\ci{+0.06}{+0.34}$) and from $0.72$
to $0.87$ at severity~2 ($+0.15$~$\ci{+0.07}{+0.22}$); the median per-prompt interaction is
$+0.051$~$\ci{+0.012}{+0.077}$ and $+0.172$~$\ci{+0.153}{+0.214}$ (Wilcoxon $p=0.02$ and $p<10^{-17}$);
on pooled-rank scores $J$ is $+0.063$ $\ci{-0.015}{+0.143}$ and $+0.199$ $\ci{+0.153}{+0.244}$; the second
scorer gives $J_{\mathrm{HC}}=+0.075$~$\ci{+0.012}{+0.137}$ at severity~1. A Holm correction over all
$13$ contrasts in this paper leaves every severity-2 conclusion unchanged (bootstrap $p<10^{-4}$); at
severity~1, $\Rn$ ($p=0.016$) and $J$ ($p=0.052$) do not survive it. The severity-1 duration effect is
therefore directionally consistent on every scale but is established by the severity-2 replication.

\subsection{At matched duration the gain is domain-specific}
Held-out music separates the domains at fixed duration. At severity~2 and $3.84$\,s the gain is
resolved in-domain ($\Rs=+0.085$) and null on music ($\Rm=+0.009$ $\ci{-0.013}{+0.032}$; $W=0.48$), a
matched-duration domain contrast of $+0.076$ $\ci{+0.047}{+0.105}$. At severity~1 the pre-specified
domain test ($96$ prompts) found no in-domain gain at $3.84$\,s ($R_{\mathrm{AC}}=-0.002$ $\ci{-0.027}{+0.021}$)
and a \emph{negative} music contrast ($\Rm=-0.094$ $\ci{-0.124}{-0.065}$), a domain contrast of
$+0.092$ $\ci{+0.054}{+0.131}$. The music penalty is severity-specific; the absence of a music gain
replicates. At the native duration the music contrast is likewise null ($+0.005$ \ci{-0.028}{+0.039};
$W=0.53$), so the large native gain is confined to the fine-tuning domain: the $10.24$\,s domain contrast
is $+0.239$ \ci{+0.195}{+0.283}, and the music duration interaction is $-0.004$ \ci{-0.043}{+0.034}.
Two artefacts are excluded. The music null is not a scoring floor: at $10.24$\,s the pruned checkpoint
scores \emph{higher} on music ($0.089$) than in-domain ($0.055$), and music scores have full spread. Nor
is it a caption-length effect: music captions are seven times longer than AudioCaps captions (median
$56.5$ vs.\ $8$ words), but within AudioCaps the per-prompt native gain is uncorrelated with caption length
(Spearman $\rho=+0.04$ $\ci{-0.12}{+0.18}$, severity~2); ``domain'' nevertheless bundles content and caption style.

\subsection{Where in the clip the gain lives}
Two post-result analyses locate the native-duration gain. First, as a prospectively frozen diagnostic,
we score the native clips with FineLAP~\cite{li2026finelap}, a frame-level language--audio grounding
model (EAT encoder, not CLAP-derived) that reports per $0.16$\,s frame how strongly the requested event
is grounded ($110$ and $49$ eligible prompts at severities 2 and 1, selected outcome-blind).
Fine-tuning raises grounding across the whole clip (severity~2: semantic mass
$+0.27$~$\ci{+0.22}{+0.33}$, occupancy $+0.27$, quarter coverage $+0.38$, peak $+0.41$; CIs exclude $0$;
both severities; seam-robust), and the gain in the first $3.84$\,s equals the gain in the remainder
($D_{\mathrm{early}}=+0.275$ vs.\ $D_{\mathrm{late}}=+0.273$; $T=-0.002$~$\ci{-0.024}{+0.020}$,
Fig.~\ref{fig:where}a): the native gain is not confined to the extra seconds. Second, a post-hoc crop
analysis separates \emph{generation length} from \emph{scoring window}: we score only the first
$3.84$\,s of each $10.24$\,s generation with the same CLAP convention and compare with the separately
generated $3.84$\,s clips of the same prompts (Fig.~\ref{fig:where}b). The crop carries a large part of
the native gain. At severity~1, $R_{\mathrm{crop}}=+0.051$ $\ci{+0.016}{+0.085}$ equals $\Rn$
($\Rn-R_{\mathrm{crop}}=+0.001$ $\ci{-0.027}{+0.029}$) and exceeds $\Rs$ by $+0.043$ $\ci{+0.002}{+0.085}$;
at severity~2, $R_{\mathrm{crop}}=+0.172$ $\ci{+0.150}{+0.194}$, with $R_{\mathrm{crop}}-\Rs=+0.087$
$\ci{+0.065}{+0.110}$ and $\Rn-R_{\mathrm{crop}}=+0.072$ $\ci{+0.055}{+0.089}$ (seam-robust). The
short-duration deficit is thus mostly a property of what the fine-tuned checkpoint \emph{generates} when
asked for a short clip, not of scoring a short excerpt---consistent with a checkpoint specialised to its
fine-tuning duration.

\subsection{Corroboration and pre-specified negatives}
CLAP is the sole primary endpoint; secondaries corroborate without gating. At the severity-2 native point
Human-CLAP~\cite{takano2025humanclap}, a CLAP model fine-tuned on human ratings (automatic, not a
listening test), gives $\Rn=+0.375$ $\ci{+0.340}{+0.408}$ and $J=+0.185$; two pre-specified event-level
metrics favour \PFT{} with paired CIs excluding zero---KL to real references $2.23$ vs.\ $4.45$
($\Delta=+2.22$ $\ci{+1.93}{+2.53}$) and PANNs top-10 captured labels $1.46$ vs.\ $0.60$
($\Delta=+0.86$ $\ci{+0.70}{+1.02}$); FAD agrees ($6.92$ vs.\ $27.4$, descriptive).

Three pre-specified hypotheses failed and are kept. (i)~That recovery fine-tuning \emph{trades}
in-domain for out-of-domain alignment: it requires an in-domain gain at $3.84$\,s, and severity~1 has
none ($R_{\mathrm{AC}}\approx0$, Sec.~4.2); the loss on music is real, the trade is not.
(ii)~The severity-1 music penalty did not replicate (Human-CLAP alone shows a small negative music
contrast at severity~2, $-0.037$~$\ci{-0.068}{-0.005}$; the sign is unestablished). (iii)~A ``late
allocation'' account of the duration effect is rejected by FineLAP ($T\approx0$). Finally, \PFT{} is not
restored to dense: at severity~1 and $10.24$\,s the dense model leads P by $+0.099$~$\ci{+0.058}{+0.140}$
and \PFT{} by $+0.048$~$\ci{-0.000}{+0.096}$---neither a resolved gap nor equivalence.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
Across severities, scorers and metrics, the recovery gain of the released fine-tuned AudioLDM-M
checkpoints tracks the operating point of the fine-tuning itself: it is largest at the fine-tuning
duration, confined to the fine-tuning domain, and heterogeneous across prompts (column $W$). The dense control sharpens the reading. At $65\%$ pruning the pruned checkpoint still
responds to clip duration exactly like the dense model, and fine-tuning adds a modest, in-domain-only
increment. At $83\%$ pruning the pruned checkpoint has lost most of that response, and fine-tuning
restores a response of dense magnitude---again only in the fine-tuning domain. This is consistent with
the recovery stage acting, at least in part, as further in-domain, native-duration specialisation. Post-pruning recovery is thus better described as multi-operating-point behaviour than
as a single restored score. We recommend that recovery studies report (1)~the
training-matched operating point \emph{and} one motivated off-native point (another duration or domain);
(2)~absolute scores of P, \PFT{} and, where available, the dense model at each point; (3)~paired gains
with prompt-level uncertainty under common random numbers; and (4)~an explicit interaction across
points---an implication of one case study (one model family, two severities, two durations, two domains).

\emph{Limitations.} Mechanistic attribution is blocked: the matched control---the dense model given the
same AudioCaps fine-tune---was trained by the original authors but no longer exists, and the public dense
text-fine-tuned release is not equivalent (different data and recipe), so the dependence cannot be
attributed to pruning rather than to fine-tuning in general. The dense duration control exists only on
the severity-1 prompt set; its comparison with the severity-2 responses is cross-set. Primary inference
rests on CLAP-family scorers (FineLAP is the only non-CLAP model) and there is no human evaluation.
Sampler settings differ from the published recipe
(DDIM $50$ vs.\ $200$, guidance $2.5$ vs.\ $3.5$, single vs.\ best-of-3); both systems are always
evaluated identically. Only two durations were tested; cross-severity
comparisons are descriptive (different prompt sets); the severity-1 duration effect does not survive
family-wise correction; the frame-level, crop and dense-control analyses are post-result.

\section{Conclusion}
For the released pruned and fine-tuned AudioLDM-M checkpoints, recovery fine-tuning recovers much at
its own operating point and much less away from it: the CLAP gain is several times larger at the
fine-tuning duration than at $3.84$\,s and absent or negative on held-out music; at $83\%$ pruning it
restores, in-domain only, a dense-like response to clip duration. A single in-domain, native-duration
score overstates what the fine-tuned checkpoint delivers elsewhere. Audio examples:
\url{https://gbibbo.github.io/audioldm-modality-swap-pruning/}.
