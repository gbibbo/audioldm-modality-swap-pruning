\title{Post-Pruning Fine-Tuning Gains Depend on the Temporal Evaluation
Operating Point in AudioLDM}

\twoauthors
  {Gabriel Bibb\'o}
  {Independent researcher \\
   \texttt{gabobibbo@gmail.com}}
  {Arshdeep Singh, \, Mark D. Plumbley}
  {King's College London, UK \\
   \{\texttt{arshdeep.singh}, \texttt{mark.plumbley}\}\texttt{@kcl.ac.uk}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Structured pruning of a text-to-audio diffusion model is followed by recovery
fine-tuning, whose benefit is usually summarised by a single aggregate score at one
inference setting. Studying the publicly released pruned and post-fine-tuning
checkpoints of AudioLDM-M-Full, we show that this benefit depends strongly on the
temporal evaluation operating point. With a paired, common-noise design we define a
temporal interaction $J$: the fine-tuning advantage at the native $10.24$\,s scale minus
that at a shorter $3.84$\,s scale. $J$ is borderline at a milder pruning severity and is
prospectively confirmed at a stronger severity on a disjoint set
($J=+0.159$, $95\%$~CI~$\ci{+0.131}{+0.187}$), robust to the pruning-seam convention. The
advantage stays positive at the short point (not a $10$\,s-only effect), and an
independent frame-level model shows the native gain is temporally broad. Post-pruning
recovery should therefore be reported across inference operating points, not collapsed to
one score; lacking a matched dense fine-tuned control or human listening, the claim is
about evaluation, not mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
text-to-audio generation, diffusion models, structured pruning, fine-tuning, evaluation
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Structured pruning followed by recovery fine-tuning is a standard route to cheaper
generative diffusion models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,kim2024bksdm}, including
text-to-audio (TTA) generation with AudioLDM~\cite{liu2023audioldm,singh2026pruning}.
The effect of the recovery stage is normally reported as the restoration of one
aggregate benchmark number---a Fr\'echet Audio Distance or a KL divergence on an
in-domain test set at a fixed sampler setting.

A generative model, however, is queried at an inference \emph{operating point}---clip
duration, sampler steps, guidance---and there is no guarantee that a single aggregate
number characterises the fine-tuned checkpoint at every operating point a user might
choose. We ask a narrow, empirical question: \emph{does the advantage of a post-pruning
fine-tuned checkpoint over the pruned checkpoint depend on the temporal evaluation
operating point?} We study the publicly released pruned and post-fine-tuning checkpoints
of AudioLDM-M-Full, treating them as the object of study rather than proposing a new
pruning or fine-tuning method. The contribution is therefore an \emph{evaluation}:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt \parskip0pt \topsep2pt
\item a paired, common-noise evaluation framework that isolates the
      post-fine-tuning$-$pruned contrast at two temporal operating points and a temporal
      interaction $J$ that quantifies how that contrast changes with duration;
\item a prospectively confirmed interaction at a stronger pruning severity on a disjoint
      set, robust to a pre-specified pruning-seam convention;
\item a multi-metric and frame-level characterisation showing the native-scale advantage
      is broad but heterogeneous, with explicit boundary conditions on what can and
      cannot be concluded.
\end{itemize}
The originally hypothesised out-of-domain specialisation effect does \emph{not} survive
testing; we report it in Section~\ref{sec:results} as the step that motivated the
operating-point question, and keep every other negative.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\rwhead{Diffusion pruning and recovery.}
Structural pruning of diffusion U-Nets with fine-tuning recovery has been studied for
image models~\cite{fang2023diffpruning,kim2024bksdm} and, for TTA, by Singh et
al.~\cite{singh2026pruning}, who L1-prune an AudioLDM-family U-Net and recover on AudioCaps,
reporting in-domain FAD/KL at a fixed sampler. These works evaluate recovery at one operating
point; we vary it and measure the change in the fine-tuning advantage.

\rwhead{Compression and inference regime.}
We are not aware of prior work establishing, for structural pruning of a TTA model, an
\emph{evaluation-time} interaction between the recovery advantage and the temporal
operating point; compression error is known to interact with the sampler step count.

\rwhead{TTA evaluation.}
CLAP similarity~\cite{wu2023clap} is a standard automatic proxy for text--audio alignment;
distributional metrics such as FAD~\cite{kilgour2019fad} and event classifiers such as
PANNs~\cite{kong2020panns} are common but sample-size and encoder sensitive. These limits
motivate a paired, multi-metric treatment.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Systems and pruning severities}
The dense model is AudioLDM-M-Full~\cite{liu2023audioldm}, a latent-diffusion U-Net with
stage multipliers \texttt{channel\_mult}~$=(1,2,3,5)$ ($415.96$\,M U-Net parameters),
itself AudioCaps-specialised by the original authors. We use the publicly released L1
structurally pruned checkpoints at two severities that prune the deeper U-Net stages more
aggressively (Table~\ref{tab:eff}): a milder \emph{severity~1} $(1,2,3,1)$ and a stronger
\emph{severity~2} $(1,2,1,1)$, reducing the U-Net by $65.0\%$ and $82.9\%$ respectively.
We verified that our pruning operator reproduces the released pruned weights bit-exactly
(per-tensor oracle checks) and we train no system.

\subsection{Post-pruning fine-tuning checkpoints}
The \emph{post-fine-tuning checkpoint} at each severity is the released checkpoint obtained
by fine-tuning the pruned U-Net on AudioCaps for $10^{6}$ steps~\cite{singh2026pruning}; we
load it, materialise its exponential-moving-average weights, and verify its checksum.
``Recovery'' names the AudioCaps fine-tuning stage, not an achieved restoration.

\subsection{Batteries and operating points}
The in-domain battery is AudioCaps~\cite{kim2019audiocaps} \textsc{test} captions; the
out-of-domain battery is a held-out set of hip-hop-style captions derived from a
MusicCaps-style source~\cite{agostinelli2023musiclm}, with near-zero exposure to that
subdomain in the recovery corpus. We generate at a \emph{short} point ($3.84$\,s, latent
length $96$) and the fine-tuning-native \emph{native} point ($10.24$\,s, latent length
$256$); the music battery is generated only at the short point. All generation uses
DDIM~\cite{song2021ddim} with $50$ steps, guidance $2.5$, $\eta=0$, \texttt{fp32}, single
generation, EMA weights. The severity-2 sets were constructed \emph{after} the severity-1 results and are disjoint
from them: $192$ AudioCaps prompts (excluding severity-1 and training prompts) and $64$ new music
prompts. Severity~1 uses $96$ AudioCaps prompts for the controlled test, a matched $80$-prompt
subset for the temporal comparison, and $64$ music prompts.

\subsection{Paired design, scoring, statistics}
For each prompt and operating point the compared systems receive identical generation noise
$x_T$ (common random numbers), so a per-prompt contrast is not confounded by sampling noise.
The primary scorer is CLAP (\texttt{laion/\allowbreak clap-htsat-fused}, rev.\ \texttt{365dea6e})~\cite{wu2023clap},
applied with a fixed batch/order/seed convention that is part of the endpoint. The unit is the
prompt (music replicates averaged first); intervals are $95\%$ prompt-level percentile bootstrap
($B=10^{4}$).

\subsection{Estimands, gates, evidence classes}
Let $R(\text{context})$ be the mean per-prompt CLAP cosine of the post-fine-tuning checkpoint
minus the pruned checkpoint; $\Rn,\Rs,\Rm$ denote the AudioCaps-native, AudioCaps-short and
held-out-music contrasts. The central quantity is whether the advantage \emph{changes} with
temporal scale, the temporal interaction
\begin{equation}
J \;=\; \Rn \;-\; \Rs ,
\end{equation}
estimated as a paired per-prompt contrast; a pre-specified gate declares it resolved when
$\mathrm{lo}_{95}(J)>0$. We also report a context contrast $K=\Rn-\Rm$; because it changes
\emph{both} the prompt domain and the duration, we read $K$ as a bundled context effect, not a
pure-domain effect. Sign gates use a smallest effect size of interest $\text{SESOI}=0.025$; a
short-point equivalence test asks whether the $90\%$ CI of $\Rs$ lies within $[-0.025,+0.025]$.
Epistemically: the severity-2 replication and its seam sensitivity are \emph{prospectively
frozen primary} (protocol/gates fixed before scoring), the severity-1 comparison a
\emph{prospectively specified follow-up}, the second scorer and KL/event metrics \emph{secondary}
corroboration, and the frame-level analysis a \emph{post-result diagnostic}. Cross-severity
magnitude comparisons are exploratory and confounded, and never gate.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Severity 2: a prospective replication}
We tested the operating-point pattern prospectively at the stronger severity~2 on the disjoint
sets, with estimands and gates frozen before any severity-2 output was scored. The interaction
is clearly resolved, $J=+0.159$~$\ci{+0.131}{+0.187}$, driven by a large native-duration
advantage, $\Rn=+0.244$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}b, Table~\ref{tab:core}). At the native
point the pruned checkpoint scores only $0.055$ and the post-fine-tuning checkpoint $0.299$, so
recovery matters most where pruning hurts most. The context contrast is also resolved, $K=+0.235$. Both
$J$ and $K$ are robust to the pruning-seam convention: an alternative baseline reproducing the
released checkpoint's decoder-seam convention (differing in three tensors) gives $J=+0.161$ and
$K=+0.238$ with the same gate outcomes. The seam alternative is a pre-specified sensitivity
analysis and never rescues a failed primary gate.

\subsection{The advantage is present, not absent, at the short point}
The interaction does not mean ``fine-tuning helps only at $10$\,s.'' At severity~2 the short-point
advantage is itself resolved and positive, $\Rs=+0.085$, and the equivalence test fails ($90\%$ CI
$\ci{+0.069}{+0.102}$, outside $\pm0.025$): the advantage is smaller at the short point, not gone.
The specific severity-1 music-negative pattern also does not replicate: at severity~2 the music
contrast is approximately null, $\Rm=+0.009$ (Table~\ref{tab:core}, Fig.~\ref{fig:summary}a). Thus
the temporal interaction replicates prospectively, the bundled context contrast $K$ is also
positive, while the exact severity-1 music-negative sign does not replicate.

\subsection{Severity 1: the path, and a borderline interaction}
The severity-2 test did not come first. A severity-1 music observation had motivated a strict
hypothesis---that fine-tuning trades in-domain for out-of-domain alignment (a recovery reversal).
Tested prospectively at the matched short point, with hypothesis, estimand and gate fixed before
scoring, it fails: the in-domain contrast is essentially zero, $R_{\text{AC}}=-0.002$ ($n=96$;
Table~\ref{tab:core}), so there is no in-domain alignment to sacrifice, while held-out music is
negative, $\Rm=-0.094$. This reframed the work from a mechanism claim to an operating-point
evaluation. Across duration on the matched $80$-prompt subset the short advantage is near zero
($\Rs=+0.008$) and the native advantage resolved ($\Rn=+0.052$), a positive but borderline
interaction $J=+0.044$~$\ci{-0.001}{+0.087}$ (CI narrowly includes zero; directional,
Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}a)---which motivated the prospective severity-2 confirmation.

\subsection{Corroboration}
We use secondary metrics conservatively; CLAP is the primary endpoint. At the severity-2 native
point a second CLAP-family scorer, Human-CLAP~\cite{takano2025humanclap} (a CLAP model
fine-tuned on human perceptual scores---an \emph{automatic} model, not a listening test), agrees
on a large advantage ($\Rn=+0.375$~$\ci{+0.340}{+0.408}$, $J=+0.185$); on held-out music it shows
a small negative contrast ($\Rm=-0.037$) that the primary CLAP does not, with no gate role, so the
music sign remains unestablished. Two pre-specified event-level metrics implemented after the
primary result also favour the post-fine-tuning checkpoint with paired CIs excluding zero: a lower
KL to real references ($2.23$ vs.\ $4.45$; $\Delta=+2.22$~$\ci{+1.93}{+2.53}$) and a higher count of
PANN top-$10$ captured ground-truth labels ($1.46$ vs.\ $0.60$; $\Delta=+0.86$~$\ci{+0.70}{+1.02}$).
Distribution-level FAD ($6.92$ vs.\ $27.4$) and FD ($48.7$ vs.\ $107.2$) point the same way but are
single scalars per system without paired CIs, so we report them as descriptive.

\subsection{Frame-level diagnostic}
As a prospectively frozen post-result diagnostic we score the native $10.24$\,s clips with
FineLAP~\cite{li2026finelap}, a non-CLAP frame-level language--audio grounding model built on an
EAT audio encoder, which reports where in the clip a requested event is
grounded. The post-fine-tuning checkpoint shows a large increase in frame-level grounding across
the whole clip (semantic mass $+0.27$~$\ci{+0.22}{+0.33}$, occupancy $+0.27$, quarter-coverage
$+0.38$, peak $+0.41$; CIs exclude $0$; both severities; seam-robust). Crucially the gain is
\emph{not} concentrated late: the late-minus-early difference is essentially zero, $T=-0.002$
$\ci{-0.024}{+0.020}$ at severity~2 (gate fails; $-0.020$ at severity~1), so the improvement is
temporally broad (Fig.~\ref{fig:summary}b). This is frame-level grounding evidence, not calibrated
event probability, local causal activation, or perceptual quality.

\section{Discussion}
In our automatic evaluation the post-pruning fine-tuning advantage is not a single number: it
depends on the temporal operating point and the evaluation context, and it
is heterogeneous across prompts ($64\%$ favour post-fine-tuning at the native point vs.\ $44\%$ at
the short point; some favour the pruned one). Recovery should therefore not be treated as a
checkpoint-intrinsic scalar.
Concretely, we recommend that post-pruning recovery studies for generative models report: (1)~the
native or training-matched inference operating point; (2)~at least one scientifically motivated
off-native operating point; (3)~absolute pruned and post-fine-tuning performance at each point;
(4)~paired post-fine-tuning$-$pruned contrasts using common random numbers where generation is
stochastic; (5)~uncertainty at the prompt/sample unit, not over generated replicates treated as
independent; (6)~an explicit interaction contrast across operating points; and (7)~a clear
separation between automatic semantic metrics and human perceptual evidence. We state this as an
implication motivated by this case study, not a universal law: it covers one model family, two
pruning severities and one temporal axis.

\section{Limitations}
Mechanistic attribution is blocked: the ideal control---a dense, unpruned model given a matched
AudioCaps fine-tune---is unavailable (confirmed deleted), and the public dense text-fine-tuned
release is not equivalent, so we cannot attribute the dependence to pruning specifically and make no
restoration-to-dense claim (the dense reference in
Fig.~\ref{fig:interaction}a neither resolves a residual gap nor establishes equivalence). Only two
temporal points were tested, so no continuous duration trend is established; the short point is
off-native and may affect other checkpoints differently. Primary inference relies on CLAP-family
semantic metrics (the second scorer is CLAP-derived) and there is no human evaluation (a designed
listening study was cancelled pre-launch, zero participants). The operating point differs from the
published recipe (DDIM $50$ vs.\ $200$), so external validity to it is untested (both systems are
scored identically). The cross-severity magnitude comparison is exploratory
and confounded, and the frame-level diagnostic is a post-result grounding measure, native-duration
only.

\section{Conclusion}
For the released pruned and post-fine-tuning checkpoints of AudioLDM-M-Full, the fine-tuning
advantage depends on the temporal evaluation operating point: an interaction borderline at a milder
severity is prospectively confirmed at a stronger severity on a disjoint set, robust to the seam
convention, present at the short point, and corroborated
by an independent frame-level measure that shows a temporally broad gain. Post-pruning recovery is
better described as multi-operating-point behaviour than as a single restored score. Companion audio
examples are online.\footnote{\url{https://gbibbo.github.io/audioldm-modality-swap-pruning/}}
